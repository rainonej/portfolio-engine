The word "simple" gets thrown around a lot in design circles. We want simple products, simple interfaces, simple onboarding. But the way we talk about simplicity often confuses the outcome with the process.

Simplicity for the user is never free. It's the result of someone — often many someones — absorbing complexity on their behalf. Every decision that disappears from a user's screen was a decision made somewhere else, by someone.

This matters because it changes how we think about our work. A simple interface isn't born simple. It becomes simple through iteration, through hard conversations about what to cut, through the willingness to hold firm against scope creep, through the courage to ship less.

The hardest thing in product design isn't adding features. It's deciding what not to add, and making that decision stick.
